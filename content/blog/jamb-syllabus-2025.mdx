The JAMB syllabus is not just a list of topics — it is the official contract between JAMB and every candidate. Every question on the UTME is drawn directly from the syllabus. Nothing that is not in the syllabus can appear on the exam. This is the most important document in your exam preparation, and most students never read it.

This guide breaks down the 2025 JAMB syllabus for the most popular subject combinations, highlights the high-yield topics, and shows you how to use PassVerse to ensure you have covered every topic.

## Why the Syllabus Is Your Most Important Study Document

Textbooks contain far more than JAMB ever tests. A typical Senior Secondary Chemistry textbook is 600+ pages. The JAMB Chemistry syllabus specifies perhaps 40 topic areas. Students who study "Chemistry" broadly waste months on material that will never appear on the exam.

The smart approach: **study the syllabus, not the textbook.** Use textbooks to understand the topics the syllabus specifies — not as the starting point of your study.

## Use of English Syllabus Highlights

Use of English is compulsory for all UTME candidates. The examination tests:

**1. Comprehension**
A passage is given and candidates answer questions on meaning, inference, and tone. Practice tip: read the questions before the passage, then scan for relevant information.

**2. Summary**
Candidates summarise a passage in a specified number of sentences. Practice concise writing — WAEC and JAMB both penalise verbosity and repetition.

**3. Lexis and Structure**
This section covers:
- Sentence completion (fill in the most appropriate word)
- Synonyms and antonyms
- Idioms and collocations
- Sentence re-ordering
- Word class and grammatical patterns

JAMB publishes a recommended word list. Candidates who study this list consistently score higher on Lexis questions.

**4. Oral English (Phonetics)**
Tests include:
- Vowel and consonant identification
- Stress patterns in words
- Rhyme identification
- Phonetic transcription reading

**Recommended textbooks:** Exam Focus English (O.I. Atteh), Mastering English for Schools and Colleges

## Mathematics Syllabus Highlights

**Number and Numeration**
- Fractions, decimals, approximation
- Indices, surds, logarithms
- Sequence and series (AP and GP)
- Modular arithmetic, number bases

**Algebra**
- Polynomials, remainder theorem
- Linear and quadratic equations
- Simultaneous equations (linear and quadratic)
- Inequalities
- Variation (direct, inverse, joint)
- Matrices and determinants

**Trigonometry**
- Sine and cosine rules
- Trigonometric identities and equations
- Elevation and depression
- Bearings

**Mensuration**
- Lengths, areas, volumes of standard shapes
- Frustum, cones, spheres

**Statistics and Probability**
- Mean, median, mode
- Standard deviation, variance
- Frequency distribution, histograms
- Probability (simple, conditional)

**Calculus**
- Differentiation of algebraic and trigonometric functions
- Integration of algebraic functions
- Application: maxima/minima, velocity/acceleration

**Recommended textbooks:** New General Mathematics SS1–3 (MAN), Further Mathematics Project

## Sciences Overview

### Biology Syllabus Key Areas
- Cell biology (structure, organelles, cell division)
- Nutrition (food groups, digestion, enzymes)
- Transport systems in plants and animals
- Genetics and variation (Mendel's laws, sex-linked traits)
- Ecology and environmental biology
- Evolution and adaptation
- Reproduction (plants and animals)

**High-yield topics:** Genetics, Cell Biology, Ecology — these appear most frequently.

### Chemistry Syllabus Key Areas
- Atomic structure and periodic table
- Chemical bonding (ionic, covalent, metallic)
- Stoichiometry and mole concept
- Acid-base and redox reactions
- Electrochemistry
- Organic chemistry (homologous series, reactions)
- Industrial chemistry (fertilisers, polymers, petroleum)
- Environmental chemistry

**High-yield topics:** Organic Chemistry and Stoichiometry are reliably tested every year.

### Physics Syllabus Key Areas
- Mechanics (motion, Newton's laws, work, energy, power)
- Waves (properties, sound, light, optics)
- Electricity and magnetism
- Modern physics (photoelectric effect, radioactivity)
- Heat and thermodynamics
- Properties of matter

**High-yield topics:** Mechanics and Electricity account for roughly 50% of Physics questions.

## Arts and Social Science Overview

### Economics
- Basic economic concepts and tools
- Demand, supply, and market equilibrium
- National income, GDP, GNP

- International trade and balance of payments
- Agricultural and industrial economics (with Nigerian focus)

### Government
- Foundations of government and politics
- Nigerian constitutional history
- Arms of government (executive, legislature, judiciary)
- Political parties and electoral systems
- Public administration and bureaucracy
- International organisations (AU, ECOWAS, UN)

### Literature in English
- Prescribed prose texts (African and non-African)
- Drama and poetry analysis
- Literary devices and terms
- Context, theme, and character analysis

Prescribed texts change annually — confirm the 2025 texts on the JAMB website.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Has the JAMB syllabus changed for 2025?**
A: JAMB occasionally makes minor adjustments to the syllabus. Check the official JAMB website (jamb.gov.ng) for the 2025 version. The core topic areas rarely change significantly from year to year.

**Q: Where can I download the full JAMB syllabus?**
A: The official JAMB syllabus is available as a free download on the JAMB website. It is also typically included in the JAMB brochure available at JAMB offices and CBT centres.

**Q: Are JAMB recommended textbooks compulsory?**
A: No. The textbooks are recommendations, not requirements. Many students pass without ever opening the recommended books. What matters is understanding the syllabus topics — which source you use is secondary.
