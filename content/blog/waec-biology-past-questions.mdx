WAEC Biology is one of the most scoring-friendly science subjects in the West African Senior School Certificate Examination — if you prepare with the right material. Students who practise past questions consistently perform significantly better than those who rely on textbooks alone. This guide breaks down the exam structure, gives you real sample questions with answers, and shows you exactly how to approach your preparation.

## Why WAEC Biology Past Questions Are Essential

The WAEC Biology syllabus has been relatively stable for over a decade. This means examiners draw from the same pool of topics year after year — sometimes rephrasing questions, sometimes asking them almost identically. Students who have worked through past questions from 2015 to 2024 walk into the exam hall with a major advantage: they recognise question patterns before the examiner finishes writing them.

Beyond pattern recognition, practising past questions builds two other critical skills: speed and confidence. The WAEC Biology objective paper requires you to answer 50 questions in 50 minutes — that is one minute per question. Students who have never timed themselves regularly run out of time on questions they actually know.

## WAEC Biology Topic Breakdown

Understanding how the exam allocates marks helps you focus your revision where it counts most.

| Topic Area | Approximate % of Exam |
|---|---|
| Cell Biology & Biochemistry | 25% |

**Cell Biology and Biochemistry** is the single most-tested area. Questions on cell structure, osmosis, diffusion, enzyme action, and photosynthesis appear in virtually every year's paper. Never skip this section.

**Genetics** has grown in frequency since 2018. Mendelian genetics, monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, blood group inheritance, and sex-linked traits are all regular features.

**Ecology** questions are often considered straightforward, but students who do not understand food chains, population dynamics, and nutrient cycling lose easy marks here.

## Sample WAEC Biology Questions with Answers

### Cell Biology

**Question 1 (WAEC 2022 style)**
Which of the following organelles is responsible for producing ATP through aerobic respiration?

A. Ribosome
B. Mitochondrion
C. Golgi apparatus
D. Lysosome

**Answer: B**
*The mitochondrion is the site of aerobic respiration. It contains the enzymes of the Krebs cycle and electron transport chain, both of which produce ATP. Ribosomes are for protein synthesis; the Golgi apparatus processes and packages proteins; lysosomes contain digestive enzymes.*

---

**Question 2 (WAEC 2020 style)**
A solution that causes a plant cell to lose water and its membrane to pull away from the cell wall is described as

A. Isotonic
B. Hypotonic
C. Hypertonic
D. Turgid

**Answer: C**
*A hypertonic solution has a higher solute concentration than the cell sap. Water moves out of the cell by osmosis, causing plasmolysis — the condition where the membrane pulls away from the cell wall. This is the opposite of turgidity.*

---

### Genetics

**Question 3 (WAEC 2021 style)**
In guinea pigs, black coat (B) is dominant over white coat (b). What is the expected phenotypic ratio from a cross between two heterozygous parents (Bb × Bb)?

A. 1 black : 1 white
B. 3 black : 1 white
C. 1 black : 2 brown : 1 white
D. All black

**Answer: B**
*A monohybrid cross between two heterozygotes (Bb × Bb) produces offspring in the ratio 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb. The BB and Bb individuals are phenotypically black (3 individuals), while bb is white (1 individual), giving a 3:1 phenotypic ratio.*

---

**Question 4 (WAEC 2019 style)**
Haemophilia is a sex-linked recessive disorder. A carrier mother (X^H X^h) and a normal father (X^H Y) have children. What fraction of their sons will have haemophilia?

A. None
B. 1/4
C. 1/2
D. All

**Answer: C**
*Sons get their X chromosome from their mother. The carrier mother can pass either X^H or X^h. Sons who receive X^h will be haemophilic (X^h Y) since they have no second X to mask the recessive allele. Half the sons will receive X^h, so 1/2 of sons will have haemophilia.*

---

### Ecology

**Question 5 (WAEC 2023 style)**
In a food chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle, the grasshopper occupies which trophic level?

A. First
B. Second
C. Third
D. Fourth

**Answer: B**
*Grass is the producer (first trophic level). The grasshopper feeds directly on grass, making it a primary consumer at the second trophic level. The frog is a secondary consumer (third level), the snake is a tertiary consumer (fourth level), and the eagle is the apex predator (fifth level).*

---

**Question 6 (WAEC 2018 style)**
The gradual and predictable change in species composition of a community over time until it reaches a stable state is called

A. Succession
B. Adaptation
C. Migration
D. Symbiosis

**Answer: A**
*Ecological succession is the process by which the structure of a biological community evolves over time. It ends at the climax community — a stable, self-sustaining state. Primary succession begins on bare rock; secondary succession occurs after disturbance of an existing community.*

---

### Physiology

**Question 7 (WAEC 2022 style)**
Which hormone is responsible for lowering blood glucose levels after a meal?

A. Glucagon
B. Adrenaline
C. Insulin
D. Thyroxine

**Answer: C**
*Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas in response to high blood glucose. It promotes glucose uptake by cells and conversion of glucose to glycogen (glycogenesis) in the liver. Glucagon does the opposite — it raises blood glucose.*

---

**Question 8 (WAEC 2020 style)**
The part of the human nephron primarily responsible for ultrafiltration is the

A. Loop of Henle
B. Bowman's capsule
C. Collecting duct
D. Distal convoluted tubule

**Answer: B**
*Ultrafiltration occurs in the Bowman's capsule (glomerular capsule). High blood pressure in the glomerulus forces small molecules — water, glucose, urea, salts — out of the blood and into the capsule. Larger molecules like proteins and red blood cells remain in the blood.*

## Study Tips Specific to WAEC Biology

**Master diagrams even in text-based practice.** WAEC Biology theory questions frequently ask you to draw and label diagrams — the mitotic stages, the nephron, the human heart, the structure of a leaf. While past question apps focus on objectives, use your textbook to study and reproduce key diagrams by hand until they are automatic.

**Examiner favourite topics (appear almost every year):**
- Photosynthesis and respiration equations
- Osmosis and diffusion practical questions
- Mendelian genetics ratios
- Structure and function of organelles
- Human digestive enzymes and their substrates
- The nitrogen cycle and carbon cycle
- Classification of organisms

**Objective vs Theory strategy.** In the objective section (Section A), aim to complete all 50 questions with at least 5 minutes to spare for review. In the theory section (Section B), read all questions before you start writing and choose the ones you can answer most completely. Partial credit is available in theory — never leave a theory question blank even if your answer is incomplete.

## Common Mistakes Students Make in WAEC Biology

**Confusing mitosis and meiosis.** These appear together in nearly every WAEC paper. Know that mitosis produces 2 genetically identical diploid cells (for growth and repair) while meiosis produces 4 genetically different haploid cells (for sexual reproduction). Mix them up and you will lose marks across multiple questions.

**Neglecting ecology.** Many students focus so heavily on cell biology and genetics that they underprepare ecology. This is a costly mistake — ecology accounts for about 20% of the paper and the questions are generally among the most straightforward if you have revised.

**Vague theory answers.** WAEC examiners mark against a marking scheme. Writing "the heart pumps blood" earns fewer marks than "the left ventricle contracts to pump oxygenated blood under high pressure into the aorta for systemic circulation." Be specific. Use correct biological terminology.

**Not practising past years from 2015 onward.** Students who only revise 2020–2024 miss recurring question types from earlier years that WAEC still uses. Cover as many years as you can.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many questions are in WAEC Biology?**
The WAEC Biology paper has two sections. Section A (Objective) contains 50 multiple-choice questions to be answered in 50 minutes. Section B (Theory/Essay) contains structured and essay questions to be answered in 1 hour 40 minutes. Total exam duration is 2 hours 30 minutes.

**Is WAEC Biology difficult?**
WAEC Biology is considered moderately difficult. The objective section is manageable for students who have practised past questions. The theory section demands more depth — you must explain processes and draw labelled diagrams accurately. Students who read their Biology textbook alongside past questions consistently perform well.

**What topics come up most in WAEC Biology?**
Cell biology (especially osmosis, photosynthesis, and cell organelles), genetics (Mendelian ratios, sex-linked traits), ecology (food chains, nutrient cycles), and human physiology (digestion, excretion, reproduction) are the most consistently tested areas across years.

**Can I pass WAEC Biology with past questions alone?**
Past questions alone are insufficient for the theory section, which requires you to explain and draw. However, combining past question practice with your textbook — especially reading explanations for questions you get wrong — covers the full syllabus effectively. Use past questions as your primary revision tool, with the textbook as your reference.
