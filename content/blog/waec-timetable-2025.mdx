## Understanding the WAEC Exam Structure

The May/June WASSCE spans approximately 7–8 weeks, typically beginning in early May and concluding in late June. Understanding the format helps you prepare intelligently.

**Paper Types:**

- **Objective (OBJ):** Multiple-choice questions. Usually 50 questions in 50 minutes.
- **Essay/Theory:** Long-answer and structured questions. Time varies by subject (typically 2–3 hours).
- **Practical:** For science subjects — Biology, Chemistry, Physics. Usually held separately on designated practical days.

**Session Times:**

Most WAEC papers run in two sessions per day:
- **Morning session:** Typically starts at 09:30 AM
- **Afternoon session:** Typically starts at 02:00 PM

Arrive at your exam centre at least 30 minutes before the session starts. WAEC rules prohibit entry after the paper has started.

## Subject Groupings

WAEC groups related subjects together within the same period, which means you could sit two papers on the same day. Here is how subjects typically cluster:

**Core subjects (all candidates):**
- English Language (Oral + Written)
- Mathematics

**Science candidates typically sit:**
- Physics, Chemistry, Biology
- Agricultural Science (where applicable)
- Further Mathematics

**Commercial candidates typically sit:**
- Economics, Commerce, Accounting
- Business Studies, Financial Accounting

**Arts and Social Science candidates typically sit:**
- Government, Literature in English, History
- Christian Religious Studies / Islamic Studies
- Geography, French, Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa

Core subjects (English and Mathematics) usually fall in the first two weeks of the examination, as every candidate must sit them.

## How to Use the Timetable to Plan Your Revision

Once the official timetable is released, follow this approach:

**Step 1 — Map your subjects.** List all the subjects you are sitting. Cross-reference with the timetable to see your personal exam schedule.

**Step 2 — Identify back-to-back subjects.** If you have two subjects on the same day, or two subjects in consecutive days, those are your highest-priority pairs. Never go into back-to-back subjects without revising both.

**Step 3 — Count your preparation days.** From today, count how many days you have before your first paper. Divide your revision time proportionally, giving more days to subjects you find difficult.

**Step 4 — Build a subject revision calendar.** Write out which subject you will revise on each day from now until your final paper. Pin it where you can see it.

**Step 5 — Schedule mock exams.** In the two weeks before the exam, schedule at least one full timed mock per subject. Use PassVerse or your past question booklets.

## Tips for Exam Day Preparation

**The night before:**
- Do not start reading new material. Light review only.
- Pack your exam materials the night before: HB pencils, blue/black pen, ruler, eraser, WAEC scratch card (for result checking).
- Know your exam centre address and confirm you know how to get there.
- Sleep by 10 PM. Sleep deprivation measurably reduces cognitive performance.

**The morning of:**
- Eat a proper breakfast — your brain runs on glucose.
- Arrive at the exam centre no later than 8:45 AM for a 9:30 AM paper.
- Keep your phone away. WAEC has strict rules against phones in the hall.

**During the exam:**
- Read all instructions before starting.
- For Objective papers, fill the bubble sheet carefully. A misaligned bubble on WAEC's optical reader can cost marks you earned.
- Attempt all questions. WAEC does not penalise wrong answers for Objective papers.
- For essay questions, plan your answer briefly before writing. Three well-developed points beat seven rushed ones.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When does WAEC release the 2025 timetable?**
A: WAEC typically releases the May/June timetable 4–6 weeks before the examination begins. Watch the official WAEC website (waecdirect.org) and registered news sources for the announcement.

**Q: What happens if my subjects clash on the timetable?**
A: WAEC rarely schedules the same candidate's papers simultaneously. If you believe there is a clash, report it immediately to your school's WAEC coordinator, who will escalate to the state office.

**Q: Can I reschedule my WAEC exam if I miss a paper?**
A: No. WAEC does not allow rescheduling for individual candidates. Missing a paper means an absent result for that subject in that sitting.
